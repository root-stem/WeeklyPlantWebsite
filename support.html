Frequently asked questions

Why 30 plants a week?

A large study of the gut microbiome found that people who ate more than 30 different plant types a week had more diverse gut bacteria than people who ate 10 or fewer. See the science behind 30 for the research. You can set a different goal in Settings.

Is Weekly Plant medical advice?

No. Weekly Plant is for informational and entertainment purposes only. Talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet. See the full disclaimer.

What counts as a plant?

Fruits, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, seeds and fungi. Each different plant counts once per week, however many times you eat it.

Wait, mushrooms aren't plants!

You got us. Fungi are their own kingdom, and they're actually more closely related to you than to a carrot. But mushrooms are fiber-rich foods that feed your gut bacteria too, so we let them into the garden as honorary plants. Please don't tell the botanists.

Isn't a tomato a fruit?

Botanically, yes. So are eggplants, bell peppers, cucumbers, zucchini, pumpkins and okra, because they grow from the plant's flower and carry its seeds. Weekly Plant sorts plants by how they're used in the kitchen, not how they're classified in the lab. If it goes in a salad rather than a fruit salad, you'll find it under Vegetables.

A plant I eat isn't in the catalog.

Try searching under another name, since the search understands many regional names, such as courgette for zucchini. If it still isn't there, let me know and I'll consider adding it.

Can I change which day my week starts?

Yes. Open Settings and pick any day from Sunday to Saturday.

My weeks shifted after I traveled.

Weekly Plant tracks weeks in a fixed timezone, which defaults to your device's timezone when you first set it up. You can change it in Settings.

Is my data backed up or synced?

Your data is stored on your device and included in your regular iCloud or computer backups. It isn't synced between devices.
