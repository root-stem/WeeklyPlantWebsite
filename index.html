The science behind 30

The 30-plant target comes from the American Gut Project, a large citizen-science study of the gut microbiome. Participants who ate more than 30 different plant types a week had more diverse gut bacteria than those who ate 10 or fewer. This held whether they called themselves vegan, vegetarian or omnivore.1

That study was observational, so it shows a link rather than proof of cause and effect. More recently, a randomized crossover trial in adults with chronic kidney disease compared a diet of 30 or more plant foods a week with one of 15 or fewer. The higher-diversity diet shifted the gut microbiome and lowered participants' symptom burden.2

Thirty isn't a magic number. It's a practical goal for eating a wider variety of plants.

  1. McDonald D, Hyde E, Debelius JW, et al. American Gut: an Open Platform for Citizen Science Microbiome Research. mSystems. 2018;3(3):e00031-18. doi:10.1128/mSystems.00031-18
  2. Stanford J, Stefoska-Needham A, Jiang X, et al. High-Diversity Plant-Based Diet and Gut Microbiome, Plasma Metabolome, and Symptoms in Adults with CKD. Clin J Am Soc Nephrol. 2025;20(5):619-631. doi:10.2215/CJN.0000000682
